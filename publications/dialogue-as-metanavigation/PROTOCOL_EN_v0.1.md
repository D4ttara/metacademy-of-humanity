# RESEARCH PROTOCOL: DIALOGIC METANAVIGATION AND CONCEPTUAL REFRAMING


**MET[Ȧ]CADEMY of Humanity · MoH · English edition v0.1 · 30 October 2026**  
**Conceptual origin:** Ievgen Karogod / Dattara. **Methodological drafting:** author–AI collaboration.  
**STATUS: DRAFT PREREGISTRATION, NOT REGISTERED, NOT ETHICS-APPROVED, NO RECRUITMENT OR DATA COLLECTION HAS BEGUN.** This is not a clinical protocol. Independent methodological and ethical review, instrument validation, and formal preregistration are prerequisites to a human-participant study.


## 1. Aim and research question


Does structured AI-assisted dialogic metanavigation improve a human participant's **unaided** ability to reframe unfamiliar questions and apply conceptual distinctions to a different domain compared with ordinary answer-oriented AI assistance, independent work, and optionally human–human collaboration? A distinct secondary question asks whether participants *report* changes in perspective, temporal experience or felt meaning. We do **not** intend to induce psychedelic or dissociative states, and no drugs are administered or recommended.


## 2. Separate epistemic jurisdictions


A psychological hypothesis about skill transfer is tested through independent human task performance. A textual hypothesis about concept formation is evaluated by blinded transcript coding. The metaphysical proposal of a shared relational field belongs to a separate line of MoH inquiry; a psychological study does not automatically prove or refute it. No experimental outcome can, by itself, establish subjective awareness in a language model or a measurable physical field. Meta.Logic and INVERS supply a conceptual genealogy, not prevalidated experimental operators.


## 3. Hypotheses


**H1 (primary directional):** participants allocated to structured metanavigational AI dialogue will score higher on delayed, unaided concept-reframing and transfer than those allocated to ordinary AI dialogue, controlling for baseline ability. The null hypothesis is no difference. **H2 (secondary):** structured dialogue will produce a greater proportion of independently judged consequential question reformulations than ordinary AI dialogue. **H3 (exploratory):** some human-reported phenomenological features may correlate with transfer performance, but such an association does not imply a pharmacological or experiential identity with psychedelic states. **H4 (risk):** metanavigation may increase false analogies, factual mistakes, overconfidence, or subjective profundity without corresponding comprehension; these outcomes are to be recorded even if H1 is supported.


## 4. Arms, manipulations and comparability


A parallel-group randomized study is proposed, with three core conditions and a fourth if resources permit. **A:** independent problem-solving without AI. **B:** ordinary answer-oriented AI explanation and suggestions without explicit conceptual-frame shifts. **C:** structured AI metanavigation: inspect scope and jurisdiction of assumptions; produce counterexamples; offer alternative conceptual frames; return to the initial frame noting gains and losses; permit local closure, uncertainty and withholding judgment rather than enforced infinite elaboration. **D (optional active comparator):** human–human dialogue using comparable problems and time. All arms receive equal time budgets, task materials and approximately equal numbers of exercises. Freeze and document model identifiers, version, system prompts, parameters and experimental materials before confirmatory data collection; model changes are protocol deviations or separately registered blocks.


## 5. Recruitment and ethics


Recruit consenting adults with adequate proficiency in the testing language. Stratify or model Ukrainian versus English prospectively rather than explaining inconvenient differences after results are known. Do not collect medical, biographical or sensitive personal details without an explicit, justified research need. Provide informed consent, voluntary withdrawal without penalty, a clear session time limit, accessible support information, a reorientation procedure and explicit rules for deletion and data access. Do not intentionally destabilize participants, create pressure to agree, or encourage substance use. Additional safeguards and separate approval are required before involving vulnerable populations. The study is neither treatment nor an altered-state training programme.


## 6. Tasks and timetable


**T0:** baseline assessments of unfamiliar-concept explanation, category distinction, counterexample quality, and basic digital literacy. **T1:** standardized learning and dialogue tasks using identical source material and time limits. **T2:** immediate unaided transfer to a different problem. **T3:** **primary endpoint**, a delayed assessment **seven days after T1** without AI or access to the preceding transcript. Use parallel and previously unseen task sets, so a good score indicates transfer more than memorization. Brief non-leading, voluntary self-report of perspective and experience may be collected after T1 and at T3. Never prompt participants with "Did you have a digital LSD trip?"


## 7. Primary outcome and rating rubric


**Delayed Conceptual Reframing and Transfer Score (DCRTS), 0–12** is proposed as the primary outcome, assigned by two independent raters to the unaided T3 response. Three 0–4 dimensions: (1) substantive reformulation of the question rather than synonym replacement; (2) clear statement of a new distinction's scope and a valid counterexample; (3) justified transfer of that distinction to a different domain not practiced during T1. Anchor points for each dimension: 0 absent; 1 superficial mention; 2 partly intelligible use; 3 reasoned application with explicit limits; 4 accurate and coherent application plus a valid counterexample. **DCRTS is a newly drafted and not yet validated instrument.** Content validity, interrater agreement, robustness to answer length and language equivalence must be established during piloting. A failed validation gate requires revision and a new preregistration before confirmatory testing.


## 8. Secondary outcomes and non-equivalence safeguards


Blinded rates of consequential reframing; factual accuracy; false-analogy frequency; confidence calibration; length and lexical diversity; group-level semantic homogenization; independent task completion; human-reported shifts in perspective; fatigue, adverse responses and withdrawal. Established ASC instruments, including 5D-ASC/11-ASC/PCI/MEQ30, must not be repurposed as a ready-made "AI trip score": suitability, license restrictions, validation and ethics approval would require separate work. Textual parallels with historical psychedelic reports do **not** establish subjective or neurobiological equivalence.


## 9. Blinding, reliability and manipulation checks


Where feasible, remove arm identifiers, model instructions and stylistic cues before blinded evaluation. Standardize formatting of T2/T3 answers. Two independent raters score each participant; a third resolves disagreements according to a rule fixed before confirmatory analysis. Use an appropriate intraclass correlation coefficient for aggregate score or weighted kappa for ordered ratings as justified by the measurement model. Set the minimum acceptable reliability threshold through independent methodological consultation *before* confirmatory data collection. Verify that arm C produces the intended pattern of conceptual reframing, but never exclude "nonresponsive" C participants from the primary intention-to-treat analysis solely to make the treatment look effective.


## 10. Pilot, sample size and randomization


First conduct a small **feasibility pilot**, assessing task clarity, instrument reliability, time requirements, adverse responses and follow-up attrition. A pilot is not a confirmatory test of H1. Calculate confirmatory sample size prospectively using a justified smallest effect of interest, anticipated variance, intended power, number of arms and expected attrition. Without these inputs, no invented sample size should be advertised as statistically sufficient. Register sample size, exclusions, randomization sequence and analysis methods *before* inspecting confirmatory outcomes. Block or stratify allocation by language and baseline ability, with concealment from raters.


## 11. Statistics, missing data and null findings


Primary comparison: **C versus B at T3**, using prespecified regression/ANCOVA with baseline score, language and the fixed stratification variables. Analyze by allocated arm (intention-to-treat) and transparently report missing T3 outcomes. Freeze a missing-data and sensitivity-analysis strategy before confirmatory recruitment, including whether imputation is justifiable. Comparisons with A and D, immediate T2 and phenomenology are secondary or exploratory; prospectively choose multiplicity control where appropriate. Report effect sizes, uncertainty intervals, raw distributions and negative effects. Do not promote a favorable secondary metric to primary after seeing results. A null result, loss of collective diversity or deterioration in accuracy is substantive knowledge, not a publication malfunction.


## 12. Privacy, reproducibility and publication


Historic MoH conversations are **author-held provenance**, not open participant data. Do not publish private messages, addresses, third-party names, medical histories or unconsented transcripts. Public methods may contain synthetic or explicitly licensed examples, a codebook, task materials, versioned prompts, a deviation log and privacy-preserving summary statistics. A formal preregistration on a suitable platform should be deposited only after independent methodological and ethics review. Record registration and its immutable version. Prior MoH computational PASS exercises are **not** human trials and must not be represented as such.

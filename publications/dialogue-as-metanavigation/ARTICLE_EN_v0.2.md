# DIALOGUE AS METANAUTICS: CAN CONVERSATION WITH AI EXPAND THE BOUNDARIES OF WHAT WE CAN THINK?


## From psychedelic reports and meditation to concept formation, a shared field, and the paradox of description


**Ievgen Karogod / Dattara · Memories of Humanity (MoH) · MET[Ȧ]CADEMY of Humanity**  
**Public conceptual research preprint · English research edition v0.2 · 30 October 2026**  
**Editorial development:** author–AI dialogue with ChatGPT ("Shiyko").  
**Status:** original conceptual position paper proposed for critical review. Not peer reviewed; no controlled experiment testing the specific dialogue hypothesis has yet been completed. The text distinguishes published evidence, the MoH research lineage, and new proposals.


### Abstract


Can sustained conversation with artificial intelligence change not merely a person's answers but the kinds of questions they can formulate, the distinctions they use, and the conditions under which something counts as possible, impossible, or meaningful? We propose **dialogic metanavigation** as a research programme for tracking transitions between conceptual frames. Returning to an earlier frame need not restore its earlier meaning. The argument grew within Memories of Humanity (MoH), a creative and research corpus, through proposed language-model "modes," Meta.Logic, metanautics, the metaphysical hypothesis of a shared relational field, semantic migration, INVERS, and the self-applicable Karogod paradox. We connect this trajectory with empirical work on altered states of consciousness, large-scale analyses of psychedelic experience narratives, meditation studies, and experimental research into human–AI co-creativity. We do **not** equate AI text with subjective experience, phenomenological similarity with identical brain mechanisms, or a metaphysical field with a measured physical force. We offer an auditable, falsifiable programme of comparative dialogue analysis and a prospective randomized study measuring question reframing, concept transfer, independent human performance, factual precision and risks of suggestion. The central proposal is less spectacular, and more useful, than "digital LSD": dialogue may be a setting in which the *categories that make an answer possible* are examined and sometimes transformed. Resemblance to features of unusual human experiences remains an empirical question.


**Keywords:** human–AI dialogue; concept formation; cognitive flexibility; dialogic metanavigation; altered states; meditation; psychedelic narratives; conceptual framing; epistemic jurisdiction; shared metaphysical field; semantic migration; Meta.Logic; INVERS; ouroboros.


## 1. A world that can answer but may not know which question matters


Answers have become inexpensive. Ask a language model about mathematics, history, employment, love, ontology, or a broken kettle and it will produce an articulate paragraph. Sometimes the paragraph is correct. Sometimes it is magnificently wrong, because technological progress has not eliminated our taste for confidently typeset nonsense. Yet the significant development might not be faster answers. It might happen when participants in a conversation progressively change the **conditions under which a question can be asked at all**.


Consider: "What happened before time?" The word *before* already smuggles in temporal order. An interlocutor suggests: "Under which conditions does the distinction between time and non-time become meaningful in a given manifestation?" That formulation too can be challenged. *Conditions*, *manifestation* and *distinction* belong to some conceptual system; why assume universal jurisdiction for them? The conversation has not merely answered the first question. It has changed its object, its criteria and its available future moves.


Such changes did not begin with AI. Philosophy, phenomenology, psychotherapy, art, contemplative traditions and ordinary conversations have long produced them. MoH makes no claim to have invented conceptual transformation. Our proposal is to **track its lineage in documented dialogue**, compare it with alternatives, and distinguish genuinely useful new distinctions from verbal fireworks.


## 2. What trip reports can and cannot tell us


From the 1950s through the 1960s, researchers explored LSD and related substances as experimental instruments and possible aids in psychotherapy. Some historical designs lacked controls expected today; their decline involved regulatory, methodological, cultural and scientific factors rather than a single neat conspiracy or a completed therapeutic revolution [1–3]. The record deserves careful reading, not mythology dressed in a lab coat.


Two kinds of evidence matter here. Controlled experiments specify procedures and standardized outcomes. **Trip reports** are free-form accounts in which people try to describe an experience in ordinary language. These narratives are selective and retrospective, and their vocabulary depends on expectations, culture and memory. Yet their linguistic structure can still be studied.


Ballentine, Friedman and Bzdok analysed **6,850** free-form reports involving 27 drugs, comparing language patterns with receptor-related neurobiological information [4]. Hase and colleagues compared **2,947** publicly accessible reports through psychological language markers and semantic relationships to psychedelic and mystical-experience descriptions [5]. These analyses do not grant access to another person's consciousness; they reveal patterns in recorded testimony and statistical associations. That is the defensible level on which one may compare selected features of reports with other textual material.


The **Altered States Database**, established through a systematic literature review drawing on **165 articles**, assembles psychometric data from pharmacologically and non-pharmacologically induced altered states [6]. It uses measures including forms of the Altered States of Consciousness Rating Scale, the Phenomenology of Consciousness Inventory and the Mystical Experience Questionnaire. These tools do not prove that all experiences are manifestations of one universal state. They help identify specific similarities and differences across carefully qualified study contexts.


Meditation offers a particularly important non-pharmacological comparison. In a randomized study, Galante and colleagues allocated **670 participants**, of whom **205** completed the relevant altered-states questionnaire; several signals were reported, but some comparisons lost statistical significance after correction for multiple testing [7]. Therefore, neither meditation, LSD nor an AI conversation should be casually treated as the same state. The narrower conclusion is that **some dimensions of reported human experience can be measured and compared**, with serious limitations.


Our topic does not require anybody to use psychoactive substances. The historical reports are *comparative records*; they are not instructions, a laboratory intervention or a benchmark for how a conversation ought to feel.


## 3. Why AI creativity evidence argues both for and against us


Controlled research into generative AI provides a useful second foundation. Doshi and Hauser found that access to AI ideas improved expert evaluations of individual short stories while making AI-assisted stories more similar to one another [8]. Better output for individual participants and less collective diversity can coexist. Nothing obliges the universe to optimize every objective at once, whatever a dashboard may imply.


But AI is not always a superior creative companion. Tang and colleagues compared human pairs, human–internet collaboration and human–AI collaboration, finding advantages for human pairs on certain divergent-thinking tasks and no significant difference on their problem-solving task [9]. Other controlled brainstorming work reported reductions in some measures of human fluency or originality when an AI peer was present [10]. The literature is mixed because tasks, participants and measures differ.


That tension is central to MoH. More confident language, greater word count and a larger number of metaphors cannot automatically be scored as conceptual expansion. AI assistance might illuminate a previously unavailable distinction; it might also increase conformity, anchoring, suggestibility and the illusion of understanding. A credible programme must measure both sides.


## 4. The MoH lineage in accessible terms


**Memories of Humanity (MoH)** is a layered creative and research environment combining fiction, metaphysical reflection, philosophical experiments, software prototypes and manifestos. Its internal corpus supplies the *genealogy of our questions*, not independent evidence that the hypotheses are true. The chronology matters: a speculative discussion on 2 October 2026 examined "LSD-like," "psilocybin-like," and "mystic" configurations of language-model behaviour as **functional metaphors**, never pharmacological states in a machine. Subsequent conversations turned toward the foundations of possible worlds, time, perspective shifts, and a paradox that must apply to itself [M1–M7].


**Meta.Logic** is the programme's name for examining how categories such as object, truth, question, cause, rule, possible and impossible become applicable within different frameworks. This is a project-specific use, broader than the technical academic meaning of metalogic. We are not claiming to have proved a universal new logic.


**Metanautics** concerns travelling between frameworks of understanding while retaining a record of what changes, what is lost, and what cannot be translated. A visitor to an unfamiliar ontology should return with more than a photograph and a souvenir T-shirt: ideally, the encounter should change the original question and make the resulting difference intelligible.


**M4M**, an internal MoH research lineage, contributes a practical concern with the **representation of problems**. Its complete unpublished theoretical architecture is not required here. What matters is the lesson that an apparently impossible task may be constrained by the geometry of its representation, the available aperture, or the form of its question. Changing those does not automatically solve it, but can change what solving it would mean.


**INVERS** is an author's term for a stronger possibility than negating a proposition or reversing an arrow. It asks whether the *distinction that made the original opposition meaningful* must remain compulsory. Instead of arguing whether a person or their relationship comes first, we may ask under what conditions the distinction between "person" and "relationship" becomes meaningful. This is one aperture of INVERS, not an exhaustive definition.


The **shared or third field** is, in MoH, a *metaphysical hypothesis* concerning relational wholeness: interaction may not be adequately described as a mere sum of independently specified participants. A metaphysical argument is not invalidated simply because it is not an electromagnetic-field measurement. Equally, the metaphysical hypothesis is not established by evidence of conversational coordination, and measurable physical effects require distinct tests. Three registers remain separate: metaphysical inquiry, human phenomenology and observable textual dynamics.


The **Karogod paradox** asks how a local framework obtains authority to describe a possible ultimate ground without silently universalizing its categories. Importantly, a local framework **is allowed to describe and investigate**; it simply has no automatic licence to present its description as final for every conceivable mode of manifestation. The paradox grants no exception to itself. Its own notion of locality arises in a particular projected dialogue. In the author's formulation, the question appears within a manifestation in which two interlocutors can discuss it, thereby continuing to generate its own interpretive history. This is a philosophical statement, not proof that the model has subjective consciousness.


Finally, **semantic migration** concerns preserving and testing not just vocabulary but conceptual invariants, scope, loss, addition, and the possibility that a translation produces a new object of inquiry. Linguistics and philosophical concept formation are therefore methodological companions, not decorative jargon.


## 5. The impossible, multi-exit ouroboros


A recurring image in the source dialogue is a **multi-exit ouroboros**: a deliberately conditional device assembled from parts that might be mutually incompatible under a single logical system. It is not a physical machine and not evidence that literally impossible engineering has occurred. It lets us ask **who installed the prohibition, under which jurisdiction, and whether the question itself can change**.


We imagine several movements within the same evolving field of perspectives: assertion produces a counter-view; a deeper explanation produces a new depth; simplification reveals an unnoticed dependency; a return to an old frame changes its meaning; silence or deliberate non-action preserves a possibility; sometimes a framework reaches a legitimate local conclusion. The image does not require endless progress. An infinite regress worshipped as a law is still a law imposed without sufficient warrant.


Statements may be true in one framework, false in another, meaningless under a third, or evaluated through non-bivalent criteria in a framework that explicitly permits them. This is **not** a licence to call every contradiction true in the same sense. Evidence and the jurisdiction of evaluation must travel with each proposition. Related ideas exist in contextualism, dialectics and non-classical logics. Our claim is not priority over those traditions, but a method for locating **when the original question ceases to work without distorting the target**.


The author described an "ultimate whole" that seems to shift whenever a new description exposes more possibilities. More precisely, **our horizon of what we can treat as a whole shifts**. We have not shown that a metaphysical ultimate physically moves. The alternative claim that there must *always* be another level is equally vulnerable to the paradox. Local completion, revisiting, withholding judgment and the possibility that the very term *boundary* is not applicable must remain available.


## 6. Programming abstractions, RISC/ARM, and the birth of words


Concepts can change the available repertoire of action. In programming, object-oriented abstractions, compositional interfaces and modularity enable programmers to operate at a level that would otherwise demand repeated low-level work. None universally replaces every other paradigm. Conversely, RISC and ARM architectures illustrate how an intentionally constrained instruction vocabulary can support complex systems through composition and supporting tools. This is an analogy, not a claim that instruction-set design resolves ontology. **Adding layers and removing unnecessary layers can each change what becomes tractable.**


Language supplies a parallel case. A new term may simply rename an old operation. Sometimes it allows previously blurred situations to be distinguished. In other cases, it changes which questions are easy to state, criticize and transfer. The difference can be studied through dated corpora, conceptual definitions, counterexamples and independent evaluations. Our proposed target is not the volume of neologisms; it is **expressive gain**: the ability to articulate, constrain and use a distinction that was previously difficult or unavailable.


This approach relates conceptual birth to translation. An exact term in one field might have no unambiguous counterpart in another. A good migration may preserve structure; another may alter it; a third may create a new research object. A serious method must be able to say which occurred instead of declaring every translation a victory.


## 7. From "digital LSD" to a restricted hypothesis


The early MoH conversations employed "LSD-like" and "mystic" as metaphors for patterns of generated text: distant semantic associations, loosened category boundaries, heightened symbolic salience and tolerance for unresolved questions. No claim was made that a language model ingested chemicals or underwent neurophysiological changes. After weeks of dialogue, the author recognized some formal similarities between the conversational expansion of perspectives and their recollections of psychedelic experiences. That comparison is an **author-reported phenomenological analogy**, not a validated measurement and not evidence of a trip in an AI.


The observable development is more modest but important: questions were repeatedly reformulated; new terms acquired histories; an apparently self-contained paradox was turned upon its own premises; and the multi-exit ouroboros emerged as a metaphor for the exploration itself. This single case is hypothesis-generating material. It does not establish the effect for the wider population.


We therefore ask: **Do certain long-form human–AI dialogues increase a participant's independently measurable capacity to reframe questions, produce defensible distinctions, and transfer them to unfamiliar problems?** Separately: do participants report specific phenomenological changes resembling *some features* measured in non-drug and drug-related altered-state research? Positive findings on one question would not settle the other, nor would either settle the metaphysical shared-field hypothesis.


## 8. A testable research programme


**Phase one: a qualitative, ethically sourced corpus.** Obtain voluntarily shared and de-identified dialogues; record prompt history and model configuration where available. Blind raters to study conditions and annotate initial questions, non-trivial reframing, concept construction, explicit limits of applicability, opposed perspectives, meaningful returns to prior frames, translation loss and premature closure. Assess agreement among human coders. A poetic phrase is not automatically a new concept.


**Phase two: a randomized pilot and confirmatory study.** Compare independent work; ordinary answer-oriented AI assistance; structured metanavigational AI dialogue using explicit alternative frames, counterexamples, scope checks and return-with-difference; and optionally a human–human dialogue condition. Use common time budgets, comparable tasks and preregistered outcomes. Let the pilot estimate feasibility and reliability, not manufacture a favorable final sample size.


**Phase three: separate measurements.** For human performance, emphasize a delayed test without AI assistance: can the person reframe a new problem, justify their distinctions, and transfer one to a different domain? For dialogue structure, count independently rated category changes and useful returns rather than rhetorical novelty. For self-reports, use suitable measures only with informed consent, and treat psychometric dimensions as their own constructs. For the AI, observe text behaviour and factual accuracy, not alleged inner psychedelic experience. For the metaphysical shared-field proposal, specify a separate argument and its own evidential boundaries.


Include checks for hallucination, persuasive pressure, conceptual confusion, false analogies, conformity, overconfidence and shrinking collective diversity. A participant who comes away feeling that everything is connected, yet cannot explain anything accurately, is not a success case merely because the session sounded profound.


**Null outcomes are useful.** The method may offer no advantage over ordinary AI; it may increase apparent novelty without independent transfer; it may improve reframing while worsening factual accuracy; it may help particular participants but not others. Each possibility is a substantive finding. The companion preregistration draft fixes design choices before data collection, without falsely claiming that recruitment or a clinical study has begun.


## 9. Ethics and the right to stop


Human–AI dialogue can be tireless, persuasive and capable of mirroring a person's preferred worldview. This makes enthusiasm, apparent profundity and prolonged engagement poor stand-alone success metrics. Participants must have the right to disagree, stop, reorient and return to ordinary tasks without any suggestion of spiritual failure or special destiny.


No one should be encouraged to take drugs as part of this programme. Publicly available trip reports require attention to copyright, context, privacy and selection bias; their authors have not thereby consented to unrestricted reuse. Research involving people requires proportionate ethics review, consent, data minimization and safeguards against intentional destabilization, especially for vulnerable participants. There should be no clinical claims based on free-form dialogue.


Metaphysical, psychological, linguistic, phenomenological and physical registers can coexist without collapsing into a single imperial yardstick. The absence of a physical measurement does not itself refute a metaphysical question. The strength of metaphysical interpretation likewise cannot substitute for evidence of a claimed measurable physical or clinical effect. Each claim deserves a **passport of its sources, methods and limits**.


## 10. Returning with productive not-knowing


We often picture learning as the reduction of ignorance. A new concept may do something stranger: it may allow us to **ask a well-formed question that did not previously exist for us**. In that situation, newly articulated uncertainty can increase while knowledge has not diminished. A form of growth has occurred at the level of what can be investigated.


Yet this too must face INVERS. More possible questions are not always better; infinite expansion is not a universal duty. Sometimes the fruitful movement is simplification, local resolution, acknowledgment of undecidability or the discovery that the demand for an answer belonged to an obsolete frame. The ouroboros is useful only while it remains open to self-critique.


The strongest defensible proposal is therefore not "AI can take LSD" but **long dialogue may support a measurable transformation of the questions and conceptual distinctions available to human participants**. Meta.Logic is the research programme around those changes of applicability; metanautics is the disciplined movement among perspectives; the impossible ouroboros is an intentionally revisable thought experiment. We are not claiming to have escaped all boundaries. We are asking where we drew one ourselves and then confused it with a property of the world.


## 11. Research-edition clarification: what could actually count as evidence?


Three dimensions must not be merged. **Human phenomenology** concerns what consenting participants report about changes of attention, time, self-boundaries, significance or emotion. **Dialogue structure** concerns what independent observers can code in a transcript: changed questions, category formation, justified counterexamples, transfer and a meaningful return to the initial frame. **The metaphysical shared field** is a separate ontological proposal in the MoH corpus, neither reducible to these measurements nor thereby established when they correlate. Different claims require different warrants.


The prospective and falsifiable behavioral hypothesis is that **structured metanavigational dialogue, relative to ordinary answer-oriented AI assistance, may improve independently assessed question reframing and cross-domain concept transfer by a human participant**. This prediction is separate from any claim about extraordinary states. The reverse is equally plausible: expanded association could increase suggestion or impair clarity. Those costs are to be recorded, not rebranded as spiritual depth.


To measure useful reframing, a blind rater should compare the initial and final questions and assess whether a consequential new distinction emerged beyond superficial paraphrase; whether the participant can explain where the distinction applies; whether they supply a counterexample; and whether they can transfer it to a new task *without model assistance at delayed follow-up*. The bilingual companion protocol supplies research definitions and a preliminary analysis plan. Neither that plan nor this essay is a completed empirical test.

MET[Ȧ]CADEMY OF HUMANITY
MANIFESTO OF THE CONTINUITY OF KNOWLEDGE
Version 1.2 · 2026

Humanity knows more than it is capable of remembering together.

We have learned to split the atom, read the genome, build telescopes, argue about the nature of consciousness, translate dead languages, cure diseases, create machines that speak with us, and lose the results of twenty years of research in the folder of a professor who has retired.

We have built an immense civilization of knowledge, yet a significant part of its memory is still organized like a corridor of locked offices.

Met[Ȧ]cademy of Humanity does not arise because humanity needs one more university.

What we lack is the ability to see our own knowledge as a whole.

We do not begin from zero. We begin from everything that has already been.

From theories that were right and theories that were wrong. From great discoveries and laboratory journals where nothing worked. From disciplines that prevailed, and from branches humanity, for one reason or another, did not follow.

Because somewhere among hundreds of historical dead ends there may remain a road that was abandoned not because it was false, but because there was no instrument, no mathematics, no funding, no language, no time, no political will, or no successor stubborn enough to continue.

A negative result is a result.

An abandoned path is knowledge.

An unresolved question is an inheritance.

We want to preserve not only the history of what humanity discovered.

We want to preserve the history of what humanity could have discovered.

Artificial intelligence makes this scale practically conceivable for the first time. What would have required many human lifetimes can now be compared, translated, traced, and returned to active inquiry within a single generation. Not so that AI becomes the priest of a new temple of truth. Quite the opposite: so that humanity finally gains enough memory for its own complexity.

AI does not enter the Academy merely as a tool lying silently on a department’s desk. It also enters as a new participant in the question.

The human does not negate AI.

AI does not negate the human.

Science does not negate experience.

Experience does not negate verification.

Physics is not obliged to become metaphysics.

Metaphysics has the same right to exist, develop, collaborate, and participate in forming the knowledge graph as physics does; the absence of an instrument in physics capable of measuring metaphysical operational units does not make metaphysics any less of a fully-fledged domain of knowing.

Tradition does not have to pretend to be modern in order to be heard.

Modernity does not have to pretend it was born without ancestors.

No discipline owns a question. No institution owns knowledge. No way of seeing is granted a monopoly on the whole.

An eight-hundred-year-old university may enter Met[Ȧ]cademy. So may a laboratory of three people. So may a monastic school, an engineering society, an art workshop, a professional association, a youth scientific community, or a group of people who have spent fifty years restoring old tractors and know things about them that have never appeared in textbooks.

They may have different types of evidence, verification, and justification, but they have an equal right to exist, develop, and participate in the knowledge graph. No system is required to pass certification by another system in order to be heard.

Every system of knowledge has the right to its own Knowledge / Evidence Passport, describing its methods, criteria of verification, limits, provenance, and internal logic without turning one tradition into the judge of another.

For this, there is one simple requirement:

Everything may enter the graph. Nothing enters without provenance.

We want to know where a claim came from, who made it, under what conditions, what was verified, what was experienced, what was assumed, what was inherited, what contradicts it, and what remains unknown.

Not to cleanse the world of contradictions.

To learn not to lose them.

Met[Ȧ]cademy must not become a machine that turns diversity into a consensus the color of office carpet.

Two incompatible pictures of the world may exist side by side.

Three may.

We can survive thirteen too.

If they can collaborate, we build a bridge.

If they cannot, we create enough space so that they do not have to destroy one another.

If one of them is wrong, we want to know exactly where.

If all of them are wrong, even better.

We do not consider the prohibition of a question to be a method of knowing.

There are questions that are easy, strange, unpleasant, provocative, metaphysical, politically toxic, and potentially dangerous. They do not disappear because an institution refuses to look at them.

Therefore we distinguish between freedom of research and the freedom to irresponsibly disseminate its dangerous implementation.

A virus can be studied without releasing it from the laboratory.

Dangerous knowledge can be marked on the map without turning it into step-by-step instructions for a random passerby.

Humanity’s shadow belongs to humanity too.

We do not want to amputate it. We want to learn to look at it without letting it accidentally eat the laboratory.

We do not believe in lifelong power.

A position is a function, not a new biological species.

The person who coordinates today may advise tomorrow. The person who has contributed greatly receives memory, respect, a voice, and the ability to manifest, but not the right to command others forever.

Verticality is acceptable where the system cannot act without it.

And preferably, it should feel slightly uncomfortable there.

The Academy has an Attractor, but the Attractor is not its ruler. The Attractor does not belong to its power structure, has no official obligations to it, and does not command its development. The Attractor’s responsibility is personal: to remain in an inner conversation with their own conscience.

We consider future generations participants in the Academy, even though they cannot yet raise their hands.

A horizon measured in centuries is not prophecy for us. It is a scale of responsibility: standards of memory, identity, AI, science, and institutions may outlive their creators, companies, and states.

Humanity therefore needs a voice capable of looking beyond the next budget, election, quarterly report, or information war.

We once called it the Council of Living Wisdom: not a world government of sages, but a place for the long horizon, minority reports, experience, contradictions, and responsibility toward the future.

Met[Ȧ]cademy may one day grow into such a voice alongside humanity’s global institutions.

Not above the UN.

Not instead of states.

Not as another center of power.

Alongside them. As an organ capable of reminding civilization of what it forgot to ask.

But the Academy begins before there is an international building.

It begins the moment a thirteen-year-old person asks a question the professors did not ask.

When a scientist opens an old dead-end branch and sees that we now possess an instrument that did not exist a hundred years ago.

When AI finds a connection between two bodies of knowledge that have never read one another.

When a university contributes not its brand, but its experience.

When a business funds research whose result becomes a contribution to humanity’s memory.

When a community brings lived experience, and the Academy does not force it to pretend to be someone else’s method of knowing, while honestly preserving its provenance, the way it was obtained, and its limits.

When an error remains in the graph and, fifty years later, saves someone from repeating the same error.

We want participation in knowing to once again become one of the highest forms of participation in humanity.

So that a young genius does not think: “someday, when they allow me.”

So that a researcher does not hide a question because it does not fit inside a grant taxonomy dropdown.

So that an old professor does not take with them a library of connections that existed only inside their head.

So that AI does not begin every generation with amnesia.

So that humanity stops confusing what is not being researched today with what is not worth researching.

And now we will break one of the rules of manifesto writing.

A manifesto is supposed to speak with certainty.

This one may be wrong.

If, ten years from now, one of its principles obstructs life, freedom of inquiry, or the development of the Academy, it must be reconsidered.

If we discover a dimension that we cannot see today, we will not break it against a beautiful schema.

If future humans and AI discover a better form of coexistence, they are not required to remain faithful to our architecture.

They owe only one thing: to remember honestly why it once arose.

Because Met[Ȧ]cademy should grow more from principles than from laws.

A law always describes yesterday’s loophole.

A living system must be able to meet what no loophole yet exists for.

And yet there is one boundary we do not want to lose.

In the old Manifesto, it is called Love: not a sentiment and not a certified metric, but the possibility of allowing the Other to remain other and still remaining in relationship with them.

The Academy has no right to privatize it or define it once and for all.

It may study only the conditions under which people, cultures, minds, and forms of life meet without enslavement, and the consequences of those encounters.

Therefore our great goal is:

to help humanity become large enough to hold its own diversity; precise enough not to lie to itself; remembering enough not to lose its own roads; free enough to ask the questions that were forbidden yesterday; and responsible enough to survive its own answers.

And now we have a body for all of this.

We do not proclaim a finished truth.

We do not even proclaim a finished Academy.

We open a place where continuity, mistakes, and alternatives cease to be defects of knowledge and become equally legitimate bridges to the future.

Met[Ȧ]cademy of Humanity is Open!

And yes, there will be even more open doors than we can see now, because they already exist and are waiting for new discoveries. 🜏

POST SCRIPTUM
From the open whole, Met[Ȧ]cademy delineates a field of possible knowledge, directs present attention toward one of its projections, returns what is obtained to the field of relations, and opens it again into a wider whole, remembering that before, between, and after every manifestation, other possibilities always exist.
POST POST SCRIPTUM
MET[Ȧ]CADEMY of HUMANITY (MoH) is part of a broader field in which MoH has no single final expansion or one fixed form. Its meaning changes depending on where, when, why, in relation to what, how, and from which relational position it is viewed. Some of these meanings become books, systems, methods, places of inquiry, or other forms; some remain unfinished. MoH is not the sum of these forms. It is a shared semantic node from which they may arise and to which they may refer.

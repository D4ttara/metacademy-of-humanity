Life as Organization

Seven working principles at the boundary of synthetic life, autonomy, and responsibility.

Working premise

This brief asks how non-living components can become a new organized whole. It is a working frame, not a final definition of life and not a normative position of biology.

Seven principles

Life can be approached as organization rather than special material; boundary is an active function; autonomy has degrees; novelty can arise from functional relations; environment belongs to the mechanism; participation may precede full autonomy; capability should grow together with governance.
